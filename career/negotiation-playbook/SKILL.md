# Offer Negotiation Playbook

Comprehensive frameworks for negotiating job offers across company types: hedge funds, big tech, security firms, and startups.

## When to Use

- Offer expected or received from any target company
- Preparing for final-round compensation discussions
- Benchmarking compensation across company types

## Company Type Compensation Patterns

### Hedge Funds (Two Sigma, Citadel, Bridgewater, DE Shaw)
- **Base**: $200-400K for senior security roles
- **Bonus**: 50-200% of base (performance + fund performance)
- **Signing**: Common, $25-75K typical
- **Equity**: Rare — replaced by co-investment opportunities or deferred comp
- **Key tactic**: Anchor on total comp, not base. Bonus is negotiable.
- **Culture**: Data-driven. Bring market data, not feelings.

### Big Tech (Stripe, Anthropic, Google, etc.)
- **Base**: $180-300K
- **RSU/Stock**: 4-year vest, often $200-800K+ total
- **Signing**: $25-100K typical, especially to offset RSU cliff
- **Bonus**: 10-20% target, less negotiable
- **Key tactic**: Negotiate RSU grant size and vesting schedule. Year 1 cash (signing + base) vs long-term equity.
- **Culture**: Competing offers are currency. Multiple offers = leverage.

### Security Firms (Wiz, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto)
- **Base**: $180-280K
- **Equity**: Pre-IPO can be massive (Wiz especially). Post-IPO is standard RSU.
- **Signing**: Variable, $20-50K
- **Key tactic**: For pre-IPO, negotiate share count + exercise window. Get preferred strike price in writing.

### Startups (< 1000 employees)
- **Base**: $150-250K (lower)
- **Equity**: 0.01-0.5% common, 4-year vest with 1-year cliff
- **Key tactic**: Negotiate equity terms (exercise window, acceleration on M&A, vesting start date).

## Negotiation Framework

### Phase 1: Information Gathering
1. Research company compensation on levels.fyi, Blind, Glassdoor
2. Identify competing offer leverage (real or credible)
3. Understand company's fiscal calendar (budget timing matters)
4. Learn the hiring manager's authority level

### Phase 2: Anchoring
- **Never give a number first** if possible
- If pressed: "I'm looking for something competitive with my other conversations, in the range of [X-Y]"
- Set anchor 10-20% above your target (they will negotiate down)
- For hedge funds: anchor on total comp including expected bonus

### Phase 3: Counter-Offer
Template response to initial offer:
> "I'm very excited about this role and the team. The base of [X] is within range, but I was hoping we could discuss [the equity/bonus/signing component] — given my [competing offers / specific value-add], I think [specific ask] would make this a clear yes."

### Phase 4: Benefits Optimization
Often more flexible than comp:
- **Start date** — negotiate 2-4 week gap for transition
- **PTO** — extra week is common ask
- **Remote/hybrid** — flexibility has $ value
- **Healthcare** — critical with baby Emma arriving April 2026
- **401k match** — immediate vesting vs cliff
- **Education/conference budget** — especially at tech companies
- **Title** — costs them nothing, affects your next negotiation

## Derek-Specific Leverage Points

- **Finalist at Bridgewater** — "I reached the final round at a top hedge fund" = validation signal
- **Round Table orchestration system** — unique technical differentiator
- **Active multi-company search** — real competing interest creates urgency
- **Security + AI intersection** — rare combination, premium market
- **Family timeline** — healthcare coverage start date matters (Emma due 4/29)

## Key Rules

1. **Never accept on the spot.** "I'd like to take 24-48 hours to review the full package."
2. **Get everything in writing** before accepting verbally.
3. **Negotiate via email** when possible — creates paper trail, removes pressure.
4. **Be collaborative, not adversarial** — "How can we make this work for both of us?"
5. **Know your walk-away number** before the conversation starts.
